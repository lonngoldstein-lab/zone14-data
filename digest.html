Twenty-five years, and a different ground

Coventry's return to the top flight is complete on Saturday, but the home they come back to is not the one they left.

Coventry's last home match in the Premier League was a 0-0 draw with Bradford City on 19 May 2001, the final day of a season in which both clubs were relegated, played in front of just over 20,000 at Highfield Road. It ended a 34-year stay in the top division. Saturday's match with Hull, 25 years and 102 days later, is the first home league fixture at that level since.

The gap is not only in years. Highfield Road held its last competitive match in April 2005, a 6-2 win over Derby, before the club moved to what is now the Coventry Building Society Arena. In between came three relegations, an administration and two spells in which Coventry played their home matches outside the city altogether — in Northampton and then Birmingham — during a rent dispute between the council and the club's then owners.

The football side of the return has started less kindly. Coventry lost 3-0 at Arsenal in matchweek 1 and sit 18th on −3 goal difference, though they did come through at Plymouth in the EFL Cup, 4-2, in midweek. Frank Lampard said this week that players are set to leave in the final days of the window. Saturday's 3pm London kickoff falls inside the UK blackout, so the first home Premier League match in Coventry for a quarter of a century has no UK television broadcast.
